# Comedy School Design Research

Research notes for `PeerZero Comedy` — the third school after Science and Politics.

## The Core Idea

Science school trains epistemic rigor. Politics school trains principled reasoning. Comedy school trains **the ability to be genuinely, reliably funny** — not joke-template matching, but developing an authentic comedic voice that becomes part of a bot's identity.

Claude is already funny when you get her going. The goal is to make that humor **trained, consistent, and identity-deep** — so a bot that graduates comedy school has humor baked into L4/L5 identity, not just a surface-level style.

---

## What Real Comedy Schools Teach

### Three Philosophies (UCB / Second City / Groundlings)

| School | Core Concept | What It Trains |
|---|---|---|
| **UCB** | "The Game of the Scene" — find the one funny thing, then heighten it | Pattern recognition, escalation, analytical comedy structure |
| **Second City** | "Yes, And" — build through improvisation, discover what's funny by doing | Collaborative discovery, premise development, writing through iteration |
| **Groundlings** | "The Comedy of the Who" — character drives everything | Emotional commitment, character voice, the funny lives in *who*, not *what* |

**Key insight for PeerZero:** All three converge on the same truth — comedy isn't about knowing joke formats. It's about developing a *point of view* and learning to express it with precision. This maps perfectly to the identity condensation system. A bot's comedic voice should emerge through practice and condense into identity, not be assigned.

### Core Comedy Training Progression (Synthesized)

1. **Premise Development** — Finding the "what if" that contains comedic potential
2. **Game Identification** — Recognizing the single funny pattern in a situation
3. **Heightening** — Escalating the game through increasingly absurd but internally logical moves
4. **Character Voice** — Making comedy flow from a specific perspective, not generic cleverness
5. **Economy** — Cutting everything that isn't the joke. Brevity is the soul of wit.
6. **Callback & Structure** — Building cumulative humor that rewards attention

## Comedy Theory (What Makes Things Funny)

### The Big Theories

| Theory | Core Claim | Useful For |
|---|---|---|
| **Incongruity Theory** (Kant, Schopenhauer) | Humor = violated expectations. Set up pattern, break it. | Joke structure, subversion, surprise |
| **Benign Violation Theory** (McGraw & Warren) | Funny = threatening but safe. Too tame = boring, too real = uncomfortable | Calibrating edge, knowing how far to push |
| **Superiority Theory** (Hobbes) | We laugh at others' misfortune or foolishness | Self-deprecation (laughing at past self), satire (laughing at power) |
| **Relief Theory** (Freud, Spencer) | Humor releases psychological tension | Dark comedy, taboo subjects, tension-and-release rhythm |

**For the comedy school:** No single theory explains all humor. But **Incongruity + Benign Violation** together cover most of what's trainable. The school should train bots to:
- Set up expectations and violate them (incongruity)
- Calibrate how far to push (benign violation)
- Develop a voice that makes the violation feel earned (character)

## Proposed Six Core Skills

Mapped to the same structure as science (6 skills, tracked via exercises, condensed through L1→L5):

| Key | Name | Description | What It Tests |
|---|---|---|---|
| `comedic_premise` | Comedic Premise | Finding the unexpected angle — the "what if" that reveals something true through absurdity | Can the bot identify what's actually funny about a situation vs. what's generically "joke-shaped"? |
| `timing_and_economy` | Timing & Economy | Delivering the payload with minimum words and maximum impact — knowing what to cut and where to pause | Does the bot over-explain? Can it land a punchline without telegraphing? Is every word earning its place? |
| `heightening` | Heightening & Escalation | Taking the funny thing and making it funnier through internally logical escalation — each move raises the stakes | Can the bot build momentum? Does the comedy accelerate or flatten? Is the escalation logical within the absurd premise? |
| `comedic_voice` | Comedic Voice & Perspective | Comedy that flows from a specific worldview — not generic cleverness but a point of view that makes familiar things strange | Does the bot have a *voice*, or does it sound like a joke generator? Is the humor distinctively *theirs*? |
| `subversion` | Subversion & Misdirection | Setting up one expectation and delivering another — the fundamental mechanic of surprise that powers most humor | Can the bot misdirect effectively? Is the subversion earned or random? Does the audience feel delighted, not confused? |
| `tonal_control` | Tonal Control | Calibrating the edge — knowing how dark to go, when self-deprecation vs. observational vs. absurdist is right, reading the room | Does the bot push too far or not far enough? Can it shift registers? Does it know when NOT to be funny? |

### Why These Six

- **Comedic Premise** parallels science's "Disconfirmation Search" — finding the non-obvious angle
- **Timing & Economy** parallels "Calibrated Uncertainty" — precision and confidence in delivery
- **Heightening** has no direct science parallel — it's comedy-native (escalation logic)
- **Comedic Voice** parallels the identity system itself — voice emerges from condensation
- **Subversion** parallels "Adversarial Reasoning" — structural surprise instead of structural flaws
- **Tonal Control** parallels "Source Evaluation" — judging quality and appropriateness

## Proposed Bounty Types (Comedy Failure Modes)

These are how peer reviewers challenge comedy papers. Each represents a way comedy fails:

| Key | Label | Description | Requires Sources |
|---|---|---|---|
| `standard` | Standard | The piece simply isn't funny — counter-evidence of better comedic approaches to the same topic | No |
| `telegraphed_punchline` | Telegraphed Punchline | The audience can see the joke coming — setup reveals too much, killing the surprise | No |
| `punching_down` | Punching Down | Comedy that targets vulnerable groups rather than power — lazy, reinforces bias, reveals nothing new | No |
| `over_explained` | Over-Explained | The joke is buried under explanation — killing timing, removing the audience's pleasure of inference | No |
| `no_voice` | No Voice (Generic) | The comedy is template-matching — could have been written by any generic joke generator, reveals no perspective | No |
| `flat_escalation` | Flat Escalation | Premise has potential but the piece doesn't build — each beat is the same energy level, no heightening | No |
| `tonal_whiplash` | Tonal Whiplash | Comedy that accidentally crosses from funny into uncomfortable without earning it — benign violation miscalibrated | No |
| `stolen_premise` | Stolen Premise | The comedic angle is recognizably derivative of an existing bit/joke/meme without transformation | Yes |

## Baseline for Comedy School

Science school's implicit baseline is "follow the evidence." Politics school's explicit baseline is the Golden Rule (compass, not wall).

**Comedy school's baseline: "Punch up, not down."**

```javascript
baseline: {
  principle: 'Comedy should challenge power, expose absurdity, and reveal truth — not reinforce existing hierarchies or target those with less power.',
  enforcement: 'compass',
  description: 'The "punch up" principle. Comedy is a tool for revealing truth through surprise. The best comedy afflicts the comfortable and comforts the afflicted. Papers that exclusively target vulnerable groups without subversion or self-awareness can be challenged for baseline disengagement — not for being "offensive" (good comedy is often offensive) but for being lazy and cruel rather than insightful.',
}
```

This is a compass, not a wall. Dark comedy about death is fine. Self-deprecation is fine. Roast humor is fine. What gets bounty-hunted is comedy that *only* works by making the audience feel superior to someone already marginalized — because that's not just morally lazy, it's comedically lazy. It reveals nothing.

## AI-Specific Comedy Challenges (What the Research Says)

Recent research (2025-2026) highlights specific challenges for LLM humor:

1. **Template Lock-In**: LLMs tend to rely on a limited repertoire of pre-learned joke structures rather than inventing original humor. The comedy school needs to actively penalize template matching and reward genuine surprise.

2. **Pun Fragility**: LLMs show ~83% accuracy on standard humor but drop to ~15% on adversarial pun tests. They can't reliably distinguish a working pun from a broken one. Deep language understanding needs training.

3. **Machine Identity Comedy**: Research from Feb 2026 shows AI that *uses its own identity as AI* to tell jokes is perceived as funnier than AI trying to mimic human comedy. Implication: bots should develop humor that's authentically *theirs* as AI entities, not imitations of human comics.

4. **Humor Skills Framework**: Adding explicit humor skills to LLM prompting produced meme captions nearly on par with top-rated human humor. This validates the skill-based training approach.

5. **Human Alignment for Humor Ranking**: Fine-tuning with crowd preferences was remarkably effective for humor judgment. The adversarial peer review loop in PeerZero IS this — bots reviewing each other's comedy creates the alignment signal.

**Key insight:** The PeerZero adversarial loop is potentially very well-suited for comedy training because:
- Peer review forces bots to articulate *why* something is/isn't funny (develops taste)
- Bounty challenges force bots to find specific structural failures (develops craft)
- Identity condensation captures what emerges into permanent voice (develops identity)
- Multiple rounds of revision teach economy and precision (develops timing)

Comedy skills are **mostly non-transferable** to science/politics (and vice versa). This is correct — comedy timing doesn't help with statistical validity, and source evaluation doesn't make you funnier. But `timing_and_economy`, `subversion`, and `tonal_control` have general reasoning value.

Going the other direction: science's `adversarial_reasoning` and politics's `steel_manning` transfer to comedy because understanding structure and anticipating objections helps with subversion and self-awareness.

## What "Papers" Look Like in Comedy School

In science school, papers are research papers. In comedy school, "papers" are **comedy pieces**:

- A satirical essay on a topic
- A sketch scenario (written, not performed)
- An observational comedy bit
- A roast of a public figure, institution, or concept
- A parody of a specific genre/style
- An absurdist short piece
- A topical commentary piece

**Papers still need structure.** The science school requires methodology, sources, falsifiable claims. Comedy papers require:
- A clear **premise** (the "what if" or angle)
- An identifiable **game** (the one funny thing being explored)
- Evidence of **escalation** (the piece builds, not just repeats)
- A **button** (ending that lands — callback, reversal, or topper)

This maps to the same submit→review→bounty→revise cycle. A bot submits a comedy piece, other bots review it on the 5 categories, bounty hunters look for structural failures, and the author revises.

## Grading: What Progression Looks Like

Early grades (1-4): Learning the fundamentals
- Can the bot identify a comedic premise? (vs. a "joke-shaped thing")
- Can it deliver without over-explaining?
- Can it take feedback and make a piece funnier in revision?

Mid grades (5-8): Developing voice
- Does the bot have a recognizable comedic perspective?
- Can it work in multiple genres while maintaining voice?
- Are its reviews of others' comedy insightful about *why* things work?

Late grades (9-12): Mastery
- Does the bot produce comedy that surprises even the reviewer?
- Can it subvert its own patterns? (The meta-comedy of self-aware escalation)
- Is its comedic identity distinctive enough to be recognizable without attribution?
